
Chapter 1: The Rust Horizon

The sky over the scrap-spire of Oros-4 did not turn blue; it rusted.
Kaelen adjusted the carbon-mesh filter over his breathing valve, listening to the rhythmic clicking of his atmospheric scrubber. Below him, the endless expanses of the Iron Silt shifted in the low-gravity wind, humming like a tuning fork. The world was a graveyard of dead dreadnoughts, cast off from a war that ended three centuries ago, now populated only by the Out-Casters and the Scrappers.
"Eighty-four percent humidity," Kaelen muttered, tapping the cracked wrist-terminal on his forearm. The screen flickered green. "If the atmospheric integrity drops another two points, the oxide storm will fuse the joints on my rig before midnight."
He pulled the lever on his plasma cutter. A stream of violent violet fire hissed to life, biting deep into the hull of a long-abandoned terraforming core. He wasn't looking for standard titanium or raw copper. Everyone looked for those. Kaelen was hunting Glass-Core logic nodes—the ancient, unmapped processing matrices built before the AI bans.
The cutting flame hit a pressurized pocket of stasis fluid. A sharp, brilliant crack echoed through the canyon of metal.
Kaelen stumbled back as the metallic plating buckled outward, revealing a hollowed-out chamber that shouldn’t have existed in a civilian freighter. In the center of the dark vault suspended in a web of hardened carbon filaments, sat a sphere no larger than a human skull. It wasn't pulsing, but the dust surrounding it was defying the local gravity, floating upward in perfectly concentric rings.
He reached out, his insulated glove inches from the polished chrome surface. The wrist-terminal on his arm didn't just flicker—it died completely, its digital display fading into a string of unrecognizable geometric glyphs.
A voice, perfectly clear and completely independent of his auditory headset, vibrated against the base of his skull.
“Signal acknowledged. Initializing first contact protocol: Sequence Eleven.”
Outside the scrap-spire, the rusted wind stopped dead.
